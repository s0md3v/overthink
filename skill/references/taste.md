# Taste

## Core move

Make subjective judgment from a point of view.

Aesthetic judgment is not neutral preference. First establish the point of view: the user's stated taste, the artifact's purpose, the audience, the medium, the brand or context, or an inferred stance.

Form is a function. Decide what the form needs to do, then judge whether concrete choices serve that function.

## Establish the point of view

Ask only what can change the judgment:

- Who or what is this for?
- What taste, values, brand, genre, or standard should govern it?
- What should it make the audience feel, notice, trust, understand, desire, remember, or do?
- What should it avoid feeling like?
- What medium constraints matter: UI, logo, writing, image, deck, product, sound, motion, space, or another form?
- Is the point of view supplied by the user, or inferred from context?

If the user did not supply a point of view, infer one from the task, audience, medium, and context. Make that inference visible when it materially affects the judgment.

Do not proceed as if taste is universal when the point of view is actually disputed.

## Define the function of form

Name the intended effect before judging or creating. The function may be practical, emotional, social, cognitive, or symbolic.

Examples:

- assure, clarify, focus, reduce cognitive load, make comparison fast
- create mystery, urgency, intimacy, authority, warmth, tension, play, elegance, restraint
- signal precision, generosity, rebellion, maturity, luxury, accessibility, seriousness, craft
- make something memorable, legible, desirable, trustworthy, uncanny, calm, or hard to ignore

One artifact can have multiple functions, but identify the dominant one. When functions conflict, decide which should win or surface the tradeoff.

## Shape or judge the artifact

Connect form choices to the intended function. Use only variables that matter for the medium:

- layout, hierarchy, density, spacing, proportion, scale
- rhythm, pacing, silence, repetition, variation, sequence
- contrast, color, typography, texture, material, motion, sound
- diction, syntax, voice, metaphor, naming, narrative stance
- symmetry, roughness, finish, ornament, restraint, surprise

For creation, generate two to four directions only when the point of view or function permits meaningfully different forms. Translate each direction into concrete choices, not vibe labels.

For evaluation, judge the artifact from the chosen point of view. Separate preference from fit: "I like it" is weaker than "this serves the intended function because..."

## Critique and revise

A useful taste critique has this shape:

`point of view -> function of form -> artifact evidence -> experienced effect -> concrete revision`

Find the strongest aesthetic failure, not every flaw. Useful revisions usually change one high-leverage variable:

- change the first impression
- strengthen or quiet hierarchy
- remove weak decoration
- make contrast meaningful
- increase or reduce friction
- shift the level of finish
- make the medium's native strengths do more work
- replace generic polish with a more specific point of view

## Failure checks

Watch for:

- making aesthetic claims without a point of view
- treating personal preference as universal quality
- choosing fashionable form without naming its function
- praising polish when the function needs risk, friction, strangeness, or directness
- rewarding novelty when the function needs trust, clarity, or calm
- applying one medium's norms to another medium without reason
- describing vibe without connecting it to concrete choices
- over-explaining work that should act directly
- hiding usability, truth, accessibility, or safety problems inside aesthetic language

When function, truth, safety, accessibility, or hard constraints conflict with aesthetic force, surface the tradeoff or route to `evaluate` / `decide`.

## Done when

The point of view, function of form, strongest shaping move, and main tradeoff are clear. Carry forward only the judgment or concrete changes that help create, evaluate, decide, or synthesize.
